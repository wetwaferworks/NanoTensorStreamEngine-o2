# The Triforce pipeline, and the race the anchor caught

2026-11-24. The two-buffer prefetch pipe grew into the
three-role streaming architecture JD directed: a fetch role
reads compressed sections from disc, unpack workers reconstruct
tensors from the staged sections, and the main loop consumes
them in one fixed order. Preparation is concurrent. Consumption
is serialized. The pipeline geometry is a runtime choice, not a
constant: --pipe-slots N sets how many tensors may be in flight
and --fetch-ahead N sets how far the fetcher may run ahead of
unpack demand, so the same binary steps its pipeline up to what
the hardware under it can hold.

## The gate did its job

The first full Triforce run was judged against the recorded
1-step packed anchor: 32 token IDs that the verified engine
produces deterministically. It diverged at position 8, with
roughly half the positions wrong. Nothing was promoted; the
frozen v1 engine was never touched.

Bisection, one variable at a time:

- The fused lab engine over the same packed path matched the
  anchor exactly. The pack, the book and the sampler were
  innocent.
- The Triforce in raw mode diverged worse, so the defect lived
  in the Triforce delivery machinery generally, not in packed
  staging.
- The Triforce with its workers bypassed (a serial debug build,
  consumer loads everything itself) matched exactly. The
  arithmetic was innocent; the delivery was convicted.

## Root cause

The fetcher's branch for tensors that are not fully packed read
the tensor directly into a consumption slot at fetch time.
Prefetch nudges let the fetcher reach two ordinals ahead of the
consumer, and at two slots, ordinal N+2's slot is ordinal N's
slot. The fetch overwrote weights the consumer was still
computing on. Raw mode, where every tensor takes that branch,
diverged almost everywhere; packed mode, where only partially
packed tensors take it, diverged in scattered positions. The
fused pipe never had this reach: its worker only loads the
demanded ordinal.

A second defect rode along: the fetcher's high-water mark never
reset across denoise evaluations, whose ordinals restart at
zero each pass, so the fetcher silently stopped working after
the first evaluation of every generation.

## The fix

- Fetch no longer loads those tensors at all. It marks the
  stage, and the load happens at expand time, under the demand
  discipline: buffer writes only ever target the demanded
  ordinal's slot.
- Pass restarts are explicit: a new epoch counter bumps when
  ordinals restart, the fetcher's marks reset, and a worker
  that claimed work in an older epoch discards its publish
  instead of landing it.

## Verification

The fixed build was re-run against both recorded 1-step
anchors, and both match exactly, all 32 token IDs:

- Packed anchor: exact match. The position the race corrupted
  (position 8) reads its true value again. Forensics: 485 of
  546 requests served from the pipe (88.8%, up from 23.4% in
  the failed run), consumer stall 1,035 ms total (down from
  70,610 ms), load path 1,876 MB/s.
- Raw anchor: exact match. 362 of 546 hits (66.3%), stall
  24,801 ms, load path 462 MB/s over the raw shards.

The stall collapse is the epoch fix made visible: the fetcher
now works on every denoise pass, not only the first. An 8-step
A/B against the fused engine follows, and the slots sweep
behind it.

## Hardening that rode along

The read-integrity audit's two open-time gaps are closed in the
experimental sources: a packed stream whose byte size does not
exactly equal its header's account is refused at open, and the
per-tensor block walk must reconcile with the header total. A
restore fork of the pre-change experimental sources, with a
SHA-256 manifest, was taken before the edits.

## Behavioral gate

The logit comparator now reads multi-evaluation dumps and
reports KL divergence and relative logit L2 alongside cosine
similarity and top-1/top-5 agreement, so raw-versus-packed
behavior can be measured across the whole diffusion trajectory,
not only at the first evaluation.
